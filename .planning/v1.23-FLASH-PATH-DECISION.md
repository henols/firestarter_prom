### 1.2 Boot modes, BOOT0 and nBOOT1 [CITED: PY32F071 Datasheet Rev 0.7 EN §2.3 Table 2-1] [UNVERIFIED-UNTIL-SILICON]

Two independent official sources give the identical three-row boot table — PY32F071 Datasheet Rev 0.7 EN §2.3 Table 2-1, corroborated by Puya UM1504 §3 Table 3-1:

| nBOOT1 bit | BOOT0 pin | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| X | 0 | Main Flash as the boot area |
| 1 | 1 | System memory as the boot area |
| 0 | 1 | SRAM as the boot area |

The PCB-relevant facts follow. BOOT0 is the shared-function pin `PF8-BOOT0`. It defaults to digital input with its internal pull-down enabled, so a bare board boots main flash by default; reaching the factory bootloader requires the strap to actively pull PF8 high. An external pull-down is therefore optional, not required — cheap insurance against a floating strap net rather than a documented necessity `[ASSUMED — internal pull-down is documented; a floating net is a layout risk, not a datasheet claim]`. `PF8` is absent on the QFN56 package (cross-reference §1.5).

The hazard this record must state plainly: `nBOOT1` is an option **bit**, not a pin. If it is programmed `0`, `BOOT0 = 1` selects SRAM rather than system memory, so the DFU recovery path is gone and the strap reaches a boot area with nothing in it. Its factory default is not given in the datasheet §2.3 or in UM1504; this record marks it unresolved and points at §9 Open Question 1 — the truth of the default, on real silicon, is `[UNVERIFIED-UNTIL-SILICON]`. This hazard, not a preference for belt-and-braces engineering, is the single strongest justification for the SWD-pads row in §3: the two requirements are linked, not independent.

### 1.3 SWD, and why PA13/PA14 are not free pins [CITED: PY32F071 Datasheet Rev 0.7 EN p.44 note 2]

After reset, `PA13` is configured as `SWDIO` (internal pull-up) and `PA14` as `SWCLK` (internal pull-down). Both also carry other alternate functions — PA13: `USART1_RX`, `COMP3_OUT`, `PVD_OUT`, `IROUT`; PA14: `USART2_TX`, `USART1_TX`, `PVD_OUT` — so reassigning either pin is possible, and doing so would foreclose the last-resort recovery path §2 names. `nRST` is a dedicated pin with an internal weak pull-up. A usable SWD header therefore needs five nets: `SWDIO`, `SWCLK`, `GND`, a VDD sense, and — given §1.2's unresolved `nBOOT1` hazard — `nRST`, which is what makes recovery from a bad option-byte state possible at all.

### 1.4 USB pins, crystal-less operation, and the HSE hedge [CITED: PY32F071 Datasheet Rev 0.7 EN §2.19 and §2.25; Puya UM1504 §1.1 Table 1-1] [UNVERIFIED-UNTIL-SILICON]

`PA11 = USB_DM` and `PA12 = USB_DP`, both typed as 5V-tolerant GPIO with USB PHY function, present on all seven packages. Puya UM1504 independently confirms the same two pins for the factory bootloader, so the DFU recovery path (§2 Tier 2) and the application path share them and there is no remap to consider.

The crystal-less argument, in four steps: this port already runs `HSE_OFF` and derives its 48 MHz USB clock from HSI × PLL (`platform/py32f071/src/main.cpp`); Puya's own factory bootloader does the same; the silicon carries a Clock Check System that accepts `USBD_SOF` as a trim reference, which is the standard crystal-less-USB mechanism; but the pinned SDK's own CTC example demonstrates the LSE reference, not `USBD_SOF`. The confidence statement follows directly `[ASSUMED — untrimmed HSI meeting USB full-speed tolerance is unproven; the SDK exemplifies a different reference]` — and therefore the depopulated HSE footprint is a correct hedge, carried forward as a checklist row in §3.

Also unresolved: whether the USB PHY provides an internal, software-controlled D+ pull-up, or whether a discrete 1.5 kΩ resistor to 3V3 is required. This could not be answered from the datasheet, the CMSIS device header, or the CherryUSB port — see §9 Open Question 2. The failure mode cuts both ways, and both directions are `[UNVERIFIED-UNTIL-SILICON]`: a board that needs the resistor and omits it does not enumerate; a board that fits one where the PHY already provides it enumerates wrongly or not at all.

### 1.5 Package selection constrains the data bus [VERIFIED: PY32F071 Datasheet Rev 0.7 EN pin-definition table, column header checked]

The datasheet's PB0–PB7 pin-definition table, reproduced in the same seven-package column order the datasheet itself uses (`–` marks a pin not bonded on that package):

| Pin | LQFP64 | CSP64 | LQFP48 | QFN64 | QFN56 | QFN48 | QFN32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PB0 | 26 | D6 | 18 | 26 | 22 | 18 | 14 |
| PB1 | 27 | C7 | 19 | 27 | 23 | 19 | 15 |
| PB2 | 28 | D7 | 20 | 28 | 24 | 20 | – |
| PB3 | 55 | D1 | 39 | 55 | – | 39 | – |
| PB4 | 56 | D3 | 40 | 56 | – | 40 | 26 |
| PB5 | 57 | E1 | 41 | 57 | – | 41 | 27 |
| PB6 | 58 | E3 | 42 | 58 | – | 42 | 28 |
| PB7 | 59 | E2 | 43 | 59 | – | 43 | 29 |

QFN56 and QFN32 cannot carry a contiguous PB0–PB7 bus at all, and QFN56 additionally lacks `PF8-BOOT0` (§1.2); the viable packages for this design are LQFP64, CSP64, QFN64, LQFP48 and QFN48. Contiguity matters because the one-snapshot `IDR` read and atomic `BSRR` write design `platform/py32f071/README.md` describes depends on the eight data lines sharing one port in one contiguous run; losing PB2 or PB3 forces either a split-port bus (two reads, two writes, no longer atomic) or a shift-and-mask. This closes the loop the seed's PCB item 3 opened — "confirm against the final package and pin multiplexing" — with a concrete answer.

### 1.6 Revision Note — three premises this record corrects

**Correction 1 — the part has a vector table offset register.** Previously believed, and stated in five places: `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` PCB-03 ("its vector-relocation implication on a part with no VTOR"), `.planning/ROADMAP.md` Phase 129 criterion 3, `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` FUT-N04's deferral reason ("Cortex-M0+ has no VTOR"), `129-CONTEXT.md` D-12, and the `BOOTLOADER` comment in `firestarter/platform/py32f071/linker/PY32F071xB_FLASH.ld` ("on a part with no VTOR") — that this part has no vector table offset register.

What is true: the pinned SDK's CMSIS device header declares `__VTOR_PRESENT 1` (`Drivers/CMSIS/Device/PY32F071/Include/py32f071xB.h:56-59`), and the SDK translation unit this target actually compiles writes `SCB->VTOR` unconditionally at every boot (`Templates/PY32F071xx_Templates/Src/system_py32f071.c:139-152`, inside `SystemInit`), which `startup_py32f071.s` calls before `main`. Both at the pinned SDK `GIT_TAG 0ed2f4b4d3391eccfd4491006a30295fd78e32c2`. `[VERIFIED: pinned SDK blobs at GIT_TAG 0ed2f4b4…, plus the in-tree CMake source manifest and startup source]`

The corrected consequence: the vector table moving is the **cheap** half, because the register handles it in one write. The **expensive** half is the one-time fleet migration — moving the application `ORIGIN` means every already-flashed unit needs a full re-flash over DFU or SWD rather than an in-place update, precisely the recovery paths a self-flash bootloader exists to avoid. This makes the case for reserving the region deliberately **stronger**, not weaker: the linker comment's instinct was right even though its stated reason was wrong.

The supersession, stated so a future reader can act on it: this record's §4 is the corrected statement. PCB-03's and ROADMAP criterion 3's phrase about a part with no VTOR, and FUT-N04's deferral reason, are **not amended by this phase** — Phase 130's `CLOSE-01` correction sweep owns that prose, and this paragraph is the citable correction `CLOSE-01` and CLOSE-02 pick up. `FUT-N04`'s *deferral* may still be correct — it cannot be validated without silicon, and FUT-N05 obsoletes it for the normal path — even though its stated *reason* is wrong; that distinction is exactly what CLOSE-02's honesty ledger needs.

**Correction 2 — `0x36B7` is not unallocated.** Previously believed, per `129-CONTEXT.md` D-09 and `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` PCB-04: that the placeholder pair is an unallocated, undocumented squat.

What is true: the vendor ID is allocated to **Puya Semiconductor** (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., and the exact pair is copied verbatim out of the pinned SDK's own USB CDC example — `Projects/PY32F071-STK/Applications/USB_Device/USBD_Virtual_COM_Port/Src/usbd_cdc_if.c:9-10` (`USBD_VID 0x36b7`, `USBD_PID 0xFFFF`), with a matching Windows driver INF, `pycdc.inf:28,31`, both at the pinned `GIT_TAG`. `[VERIFIED: pinned SDK blobs]` covers the provenance; `[CITED: the-sz.com USB ID database, accessed 2026-08-02]` covers the allocation holder, noted as single-source and corroborated only circumstantially — absence from the community-maintained `linux-usb.org/usb.ids` is not evidence of non-allocation.

The consequence, in one sentence: the board currently presents another company's registered vendor identity, which is worse than a collision risk. §5 carries the decision this correction hands off to; it is not restated here.

**Correction 3 — the seed's bootloader size is optimistic.** The seed says a small bootloader in the first few KB of the 128 KiB flash. Measured against real objects, the components a CDC + COBS self-flash bootloader must carry already total roughly **14.6 KiB** before any bootloader logic exists. This record supersedes that number specifically; the seed remains live for FUT-N05 in every other respect, and §4 carries the replacement figure with its cost attached.

## 2. Decision — the three-tier flash path [SHARED:S1]

This record fixes three flash-path routes onto a PY32F071 board, in priority order, each with a fixed role: a **self-flash bootloader** as the intended primary path, **factory USB DFU** as the maintainer/manufacturing recovery route, and **SWD** as the last resort.

**Tier 1 — self-flash bootloader over the existing USB CDC + COBS transport. Intended primary.** The host sends the new firmware image over the serial port it already owns, using the same USB CDC + COBS framing the application firmware already speaks; a small bootloader in the reserved region (§4) writes the image to the application region. Zero new host dependencies: `pyserial` is already a `firestarter_app` dependency, so no binary discovery, no `avrdude.conf` analogue, and no driver install are needed — structurally identical to how the Arduino Uno's bootloader works today. This removes both the libusb/WinUSB driver friction of Tier 2 and the BOOT0 strap dance. It is **not built in v1.23**: it is FUT-N05, its own milestone, and §4 records the flash budget it will need. It carries three reliability properties from the seed, each `[UNVERIFIED-UNTIL-SILICON]` because they describe the intended behaviour of software that does not exist on hardware that does not exist: the bootloader is never in its own update path; the application image is CRC-verified before the jump; and an interrupted transfer leaves the board in the bootloader, a recoverable state rather than a brick.

**Tier 2 — factory USB DFU. Maintainer/manufacturing recovery.** The part's own factory bootloader, resident in system memory, reached over the same two USB pins as the application (§1.4), entered by the strap condition §1.2 describes. v1.23 landed a pure-Python DFU client, `firestarter_app/firestarter/py32_dfu.py`, so no external binary is required; the residual cost is `pyusb` plus a libusb backend, and a WinUSB driver on Windows. Reaching this tier depends on the strap, and the strap depends on an option bit whose factory value §1.2 records as unresolved.

**Tier 3 — SWD. Last resort.** On PA13/PA14 plus `nRST` (§1.3). Unconditional: it requires physical board access and a probe, and it is the only route that survives a bad option-byte state.

External tools are acceptable on the recovery path — Tier 2 and Tier 3 belong to the maintainer and the factory — but never on the end-user path, which is the operator constraint the seed records and this record inherits.

**Landing the factory USB DFU path in v1.23 does not retire the self-flash bootloader seed.** The DFU path shortens the road to the bootloader rather than replacing it: it proves the transfer sequence over real USB, while remaining the recovery route the PCB requirements in §3 already assume.
